Getting started with Quartermaster

Balise Studio · For testers · Updated 5 October 2026 (build 67) · What's new

Quartermaster plans your day in one place: meals and batch cooking, habits, workouts, projects and, if you like, chores with the people you live with. Everything is saved on your phone, and the extras are all optional.

Installing as a tester

  1. Open the testing invitation link you were sent, on your Android phone, and tap Accept invitation.
  2. Tap Download it on Google Play and install Quartermaster.
  3. Updates arrive through the Play Store like any other app. When a new one is out, Today shows Update ready: tap Update and it downloads while you carry on, then Restart to finish. If one doesn't show, force-close the Play Store app and look again.

You need Android 8 or newer, and a Play Store account that's on the testers list.

Your first five minutes

  1. Start fresh for your own data, or Explore with sample data to look around first. You can switch later in Settings → Your data.
  2. Choose the features you want. The essentials start switched on; anything you switch off disappears, and you can turn it back on any time.
  3. Open Settings (the cog, top right) → Goals and set your daily targets. Suggest a starting point works one out from your height, weight and activity.
  4. Tap the big + and log something you've eaten. Search for it, scan the barcode, or type it in.
  5. On Today, the Getting started card ticks itself off as you go.
  6. Starting fresh, you begin with no habits. The Habits card on Today offers a few ideas to add with one tap, or Add your own.

The tabs

Today

Your numbers for the day (calories, protein, water, steps or today's workout), habits, Today's meals, Today's 1 Thing with a focus timer, and what's next. Today's meals lists what's planned for each meal today: tick one to log it (batch portions come out of stock, and the toast has Undo). Meals with nothing planned are left out; Log something else opens the log sheet. If a fridge batch needs eating today or tomorrow and isn't planned, the card says so. Start Day and Close Day bookend it: the buttons at the bottom show when they're useful (Close Day from 5pm, Week from Friday to Monday, or any day from Settings → Schedule). Tap Arrange cards at the bottom to reorder or hide cards. Look back: tap the arrow next to the date under Today for yesterday, or tap the date to pick any past day from a calendar; Today then shows that day, and you can add or change meals, water, movement, weight and habits for it. Switch between Day, Week, Month and 3 months at the top. Reports (next to the date, once you've a few days logged) shows a week, a month or three months: your averages, best day and streaks, your workouts by type, and how they compare with the period before, all worked out on your phone. A month report adds your week-by-week numbers and a three-month report your month-by-month ones (average calories and protein on days you logged food, habits done and workouts). On a Monday Today offers last week's report, and on the 1st last month's. How was today? From 6pm a card asks how your day went (and your energy, if you skipped Start Day); tap Not today to hide it. Your mood shows in Close the Week, your reports and the Recovery card under Health. Habits, chores and Up next fold to one line when everything's done or there's nothing due; tap the arrow on a card to fold or open it yourself. In Settings → Display you can choose how the numbers at the top look (rows, compact tiles, rings or big tiles) and show habits as rings: tap a ring to tick it, or press and hold it to tick or undo any part. Tapping a full ring opens the same panel, where One more logs extra beyond your goal (it shows as +1). After-meal habits can be ticked before you log the meal.

Habits (Settings → Habits) can be every day, several times a day (tap + for each one, like glasses of water), after meals, a set number of times a week or month on any days, one day a week, every few days, weeks or months, or on a date each month. Tap Not today under a habit to put it off without breaking your streak.

Timed habits: for an every-day or several-times-a-day habit with a length, tick Use a timer. Tap the habit to start the timer and again to stop it. Each length counts as one, so reading for two hours in one go covers two one-hour sessions, and extra time shows as a plus. When a session's time is up, Quartermaster asks Keep going? (on screen, or with buttons on the notification). If you don't answer within 15 minutes, it stops at the end of that session, so a forgotten timer doesn't overcount. Got it wrong? Tap Edit time to set today's minutes.

Plan

  • Schedule: your day, including Google Calendar events if you connect it. Tap a task's name to rename it or change its time and length (on Today and in Start Day too). Tap a block's time to move it, or press and hold and drag it up or down.
  • Work hours: in Settings → Schedule, set the days and hours you work, different hours for any day, lunch (counted as free time) and how long your commute takes. Chores, habits and other tasks are then fitted around work, and tasks marked Work task (or in a project ticked Work project) inside it. You can still put anything at any time yourself. Day off? Tick Not working today in Start Day. If Google Calendar is linked, an all-day holiday or annual leave, an out-of-office or a bank holiday (from a holidays calendar you show) counts as a day off by itself; untick Not working today if you're working anyway, or turn off Days off from Google Calendar in the same settings.
  • Meals: the meal plan (7 days, 2 weeks, this month or up to a date: tap the range at the top) and your fridge and freezer batch stock. Auto-fill plans from what you have, nearest its use-by date first, and the app reminds you to defrost and freeze. Each batch shows when to eat it by: three days from cooking in the fridge, a day once defrosted, and about three months in the freezer (shown as Best by). Tap the date to set your own from the label, or clear it to go back to the usual.
  • Planning a meal: tap a meal on any day to add a batch meal, a portion of a saved recipe (set how many you're eating and cooking for) and sides such as a salad or garlic bread. Planned recipes go on the shopping list; after logging one cooked for guests, you can put the leftovers into stock.
  • Recipes: tap + → Recipe (or the + on Plan → Meals) and paste a link, or tap Share → Quartermaster on a recipe page in Chrome. Choose how many portions you're making and the ingredients scale. Under Eat it, pick a day, a meal and how many are eating to put it straight on the meal plan. Save + shopping list adds the amounts to your list; Cooked it adds the batch to your stock.
  • Your own recipes: Add a recipe → Make one from scratch. Name it, set the portions, and add each ingredient by searching or tapping Scan to read a pack's barcode.
  • Your recipe book: Plan → Meals → Recipes. Search by name or ingredient and tap one to open it. Switch its ingredients between As written and A–Z; the app remembers which you like.
  • Cook mode: open a recipe and tap Cook mode. It shows the ingredients to get out first, then the method one step at a time in big text, and keeps the screen on. When a step mentions a time (simmer for 20 minutes), tap Start a timer; it rings even if you leave the app.
  • Sharing a recipe: open a saved recipe. Send… sends it as text to anyone through your phone's share sheet. Share with your household or a friends group puts a copy in that group: they'll see it under Recipes and can tap Save to keep their own copy (changes to yours don't change theirs).
  • Auto-fill: uses your batch stock first, then your recipes for the meals they're tagged for (Eat it for on the recipe). It plans the cook and the leftovers, and keeps each day under your calorie target. It also tries to use things up: a recipe that shares fresh ingredients with something you've just cooked comes first, with a note saying what it uses up. Set Leftovers on the recipe: Freezes well spreads them across the week, Keeps 2 days uses them in the next day or two, and Best fresh cooks just the one portion. The shopping list buys for the cook only. Choose how far ahead it shops under Shop for.
  • Today's meals in Start Day: pick from your batch stock or your recipes for each meal. For a recipe, set how many you're eating and cooking for; auto-fill then plans the spare portions as leftovers over the next few days (the same goes for a cook you plan on Plan → Meals).
  • Ate one of your recipes without planning it? + → Meal → Recipe: pick it and how many portions you had.
  • Batch stock buttons: Ate one logs a portion as eaten; Remove one takes a portion out without logging it (given away or binned). Tap the meals under a dish's name (Lunch · Dinner) to change which meals it's for.
  • Cooked one? Tap Cooked it on the recipe, or Batch on Batch stock and pick it under Start from one of your recipes. Its portions go into your stock.
  • Fixing an ingredient: if a recipe picks the wrong thing (tinned beans instead of dried, say), tap the ingredient's name and search for the right one: your foods come first, then the basics, then Open Food Facts. Or tap Create my own and enter the values per 100g from the pack. Quartermaster remembers your fix for next time. Change the amount and unit (g, spoons, tins, each) under each ingredient, tap × to remove one, or Add an ingredient.
  • Projects: break bigger jobs into tasks and plan them onto days. Each project adds up its tasks' times: the time left shows under its name, and opening it shows the total, how much is done and how much is to go (a task with no time set counts as 30 minutes). Tap the tick box to tick a step off. Tap (or press and hold) the step itself to change it in a pop-up: rename it, set its time, plan its day, delete it, or choose Do after to make it wait for another task (add another step after it, then choose Needs all of these when every one has to be done first, or Any one of these when it can follow either; tick Either this or … when only one is needed). The tick on the number keyboard saves. Add what comes next builds a chain, with suggestions for things like washing. Next steps join their step's day when they fit into it after that step; otherwise plan them yourself, and tap Plan on a step to give it a day. Tap a planned step's day (Today, say) to take it off that day; Undo puts it back. Add a step opens the same pop-up for a new one. Choose when the next step is Ready: straight after, a while after (say 2 hours, for a second coat of paint) or the next day. A waiting task stays off your day until the one before it is done and its wait is over (for a step that needs all of several, the wait starts when the last one is ticked), and your phone reminds you when it's ready. Switch a project to Timeline to see each step on the day it could happen, in order and around your free time and work hours, and when the whole project could be finished. Steps you've planned stay where you put them. On a task on today's schedule, tap it and choose Add what comes next: if it was just for today, it moves to Later (still on today) so the chain keeps.
  • Later: tasks with no day yet. Add one from + → Task → Later, or tap Later when editing a task you won't get to today. Tap Plan on one to give it a day, or pull it in at Start Day.
  • Coming up: the next 7 days at a glance (tap Further for 3 weeks): planned movement and cooks, project tasks with a day, and household chores that are due.
  • Any date: wherever you pick a day, Other date… lets you choose one further ahead.

Health

Steps, gym sessions (sets, reps, personal bests and a rest timer), runs, rides and swims, distance, sleep and weight, plus trends. If you connect Health Connect and allow Workouts, sessions from your watch or another app are added for you, and matching ones you've already logged aren't doubled up.

Home

Your household and friends groups. See below.

The + button

Everything you log during the day. Food comes first (Meal, Recipe, Batch cook), then Movement, Water, Weight and Task, plus Chore if you're in a household. Movement and Batch cook ask whether you've done it or want to plan it for later. Task asks whether it's for today, Later (no day yet) or a project. Missed a day? Tap the date under Today (or Missed logging something? under Plan → Schedule → Coming up) and pick the day.

Swipe left and right to move between tabs. Android's back button always takes you to Today.

Your week

Close the Week reviews the whole week, Monday to Sunday: close it on Sunday evening or on Monday for the week just ended. The first time you open the app in a new week, a recap shows how last week went and who won each household or friends group. You can turn it off, along with the vibration on ticks and the animations, in Settings → Display.

Light or dark

The app is dark to start. In Settings → Display → Theme pick Light, or Match phone to follow your phone's dark mode setting (it checks again each time you come back to the app). The home-screen widgets have their own setting under Home-screen widgets: the same as the app, or always dark, light or matching your phone.

Household & friends

  1. Open the Home tab, type the name you'd like others to see, and tap New household (or New friends group).
  2. Tap Invite someone and send the six-letter code. It lasts seven days.
  3. The other person opens Home, taps Join with a code, and enters it.
  • Chores can repeat daily, on set days, a number of times a week or month (they stay in the list until they're done that many times), every few days, weeks or months, or on a date each month. They can be for anyone, one person or take turns, and they earn points. Not today puts one off until tomorrow.
  • Follow-ups: some chores lead to another, like loading then emptying the dishwasher. When you add one, Quartermaster suggests the follow-up and how long to wait; tick it to add it, or pick After another chore under How often. The follow-up appears once the first is done, and the person who did it gets a reminder when it's ready.
  • Chains and either/or: tick a follow-up and its own next steps appear under it, so you can build Wash darks → Hang out the washing or Tumble dry → Fold and put away in one go. Hang out and tumble dry are either/or: tick one off and the other drops away. Fold and put away follows whichever you did. Follow-ups that are waiting are listed under follow-ups waiting on the Chores card, where you can change or delete them.
  • Planning a chore: tap Plan on a chore, choose who's doing it, the day and (if you like) a time. Yours go on your schedule, and ticking one there ticks it off for everyone. Choose someone else to give it to them. Everyone sees "Sam's doing this at 18:00" in their colour. Start Day also lists today's chores with Add to my day.
  • Joint projects: make a project For your household or a friends group in Plan → Projects, or open one of your own projects and tap Share. Planning one of its tasks tells everyone you're on it.
  • Shopping is one list on everyone's phone. Plan → Meals → Shopping list shows your batch-cook items and the household's list together: items with a house are shared, and you can add your batch-cook items to the household list in one tap.
  • Your colour: tap your own name on the members card to pick the colour others see you in.
  • Leaderboards reset every Monday. Turn off Share my weekly numbers in a group to keep your counts to yourself.
  • Challenges: tap New on the Challenges card, choose what to count (swims, distance, workouts, chore points and more), set the finish line (this week, this month or the next 7 to 30 days) and, if you like, a target such as "first to 10 swims". When it finishes, everyone sees who won.

Only what you put in a group is shared.

Removing someone or reporting a problem: whoever created a group can remove a member by tapping the × next to their name. To report anything inappropriate in a group, tap Report a problem at the bottom of the group's card; it emails us with the group's details.

Keep your groups after a reinstall: in Settings → Your data, tap Link to Google. If you reinstall or change phone, open the Home tab and tap Sign in to get your groups back. Without a link, ask someone in the group for a new code to rejoin.

Connections (all optional)

Google Calendar stays connected once you've signed in: Quartermaster renews its access in the background. If Google ever needs you (for example after you remove access), a note appears under Google in Settings; tap Sync now when it suits you.

  • Google Calendar: Settings → Integrations. Events come into your schedule, and blocks you move or tick update in Calendar.
  • Health Connect: Settings → Integrations. Steps, distance, sleep, weight and workouts from your phone or watch. Quartermaster only reads; it never writes to Health Connect.
  • Reminders: Settings → Reminders. Nudges to log meals, drink water, close the day or weigh in, Chores running out of days for chores you do a few times a week or month (only when one is getting tight), and Food to use up when a fridge batch is on its last day and isn't planned. Each skips itself if you've already done it.
  • Widgets: long-press your home screen → Widgets → Quartermaster. There are widgets for today, water, quick logging, up next, this week, meals, the gym, and today's habits and chores. On the habits and chores widget, tap one (anywhere on its row) to tick it: it shows as done straight away and is added to the app next time you open it (timed habits open the app instead). Long-press the app icon for shortcuts.

Your data

Everything is stored on your phone. Use Settings → Your data → Export backup now and then, and save it somewhere safe such as Google Drive. Read the full privacy policy.

Two optional ways to help shape the app: tap the lightbulb on a habit or saved recipe to suggest it as a preset, or turn on Help improve Quartermaster in Settings → Your data (it shows exactly what would be sent first).

Feedback and bugs

Please tell us what works and what doesn't. While Quartermaster is in testing, a small card on Today asks now and then (first after three days); Not now puts it off for a week. Email balisestudio.dev@gmail.com, or tap Send feedback in the app (on the What's new card, or Settings → Help), which adds the build number and your phone model for you. A screenshot or screen recording helps a lot.

If a screen ever hits a bug, you'll see Something went wrong instead of a blank screen. Try again usually gets you going; Send report emails us the error so we can fix it. Your data is safe on your phone either way.
